The diptych logic. Plate I (60%) is the illustration — Harbour Town as we hold it in our heads. Plate II (40%) is the photograph — Hilton Head as it actually is on a given morning. Together they read as a magazine plate, not as "two options for the same hero." Hairline gold separator. Italic Fraunces captions tie the panels back to each other.

Production path. Plate I stays as an illustration (commission Lowcountry illustrator at higher fidelity). Plate II becomes a real Hilton Head photo (licensed or Unsplash-with-discipline). The frame holds either way; only the assets change.

Why this works. Two of the most-cited luxury references — Aman's monthly journal layouts and Belmond's destination cover pages — use exactly this dual-medium device. It signals editorial care without being precious about it.
