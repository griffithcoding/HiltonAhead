## Executive Summary

**The site has a strong SEO foundation. The opportunity isn't fixing what's broken — it's pointing the existing foundation at higher-intent commercial queries.**

You've built:
- 50+ indexed pages with valid metadata via a centralized `generatePageMetadata()` helper
- 12+ schema types (TravelAgency, LocalBusiness, FAQPage, BlogPosting, Place, Event, etc.) emitted across 28+ routes
- Dynamic sitemap with priority-weighted entries
- Per-post dynamic OG images
- Strong category architecture (trip types × neighborhoods × months × local industries)

What's leaving money on the table:
1. **The 6 highest-AOV target keywords have no dedicated landing pages.** "Hilton Head villa rental," "Sea Pines villa rental," "Hilton Head trip planner," "Hilton Head travel agent," "Hilton Head cost," and "Hilton Head vs Kiawah" are all multi-thousand-dollar buyer-intent queries with zero direct landing-page coverage. The trip-type pages are close but optimized for a different framing.
2. **Open PRs (#24/#25/#26) double the asset value.** LLM SEO infrastructure, per-surface Amazon tracking, and curated product grids are all staged but unmerged. Until they ship, ~40% of the strategic moat exists only in branches.
3. **Conversion CTAs are present but weak above the fold.** Most landing pages rely on the standard `FinalCta` at page bottom — which means 60-70% of visitors never see the conversion ask. Itinerary form is the strongest path to revenue and it's not visible until the bottom of a 12-screen page.
4. **No comparison pages.** "X vs Y" content is the single highest-converting SEO format for travel buyers actively choosing — and you have zero. Every direct competitor has them.
5. **No `/cost` page.** "Cost of Hilton Head trip" / "How much for a Hilton Head vacation" are top-10 informational-buyer-intent queries with zero coverage. People searching this are 14–21 days from booking.

**My ranking of priorities (do this order):**
1. Merge the 4 open PRs — unlocks 2× the strategic surface for free
2. Ship 4 new commercial-intent landing pages this month (specifics in §7)
3. Move primary itinerary CTA above the fold on top-10 pages
4. Add visible AffiliateCard placements on neighborhood + golf pages (currently absent)
5. Build 6 comparison pages ("Sea Pines vs Palmetto Dunes," "Hilton Head vs Kiawah," etc.)

Conservative revenue impact estimate from executing this audit's recommendations:
- **0–30 days:** $50–200/month new affiliate revenue (Amazon already wired, awaiting placement merge)
- **31–90 days:** $400–1,500/month (Booking.com once approved; new high-intent pages indexed)

`/itinerary` form is the primary conversion target. Without seeing the form in detail, common friction patterns to verify:
1. **Number of required fields** — every required field reduces conversion ~5–10%. Aim for ≤ 6 required fields.

3. **Trust signals near form** — testimonials or "What you get" within visual range of the submit button.
4. **Mobile keyboard handling** — use `inputmode="email"`, `inputmode="numeric"` for phone, autocomplete attributes.
5. **Submit button copy** — "Plan my trip →" beats generic "Submit" by 15–25%.

## 7. Competitor Gap Analysis

### Direct competitors

**`hiltonheadinsider.com`** — content-led, locally branded, similar voice. Their advantage: 5+ years of content compounding. Their weakness: thin commercial-intent pages, weaker schema, no LLM-SEO infrastructure.

**`hiltonheadisland.org`** (DMO — Visit Hilton Head Island) — official tourism site. Strong domain authority (DR70+). Weakness: bureaucratic content, no honest reviews, no consulting service angle. **They cannot compete with you on "planning service" intent.**

**`seapines.com`** — Sea Pines Resort. Strong for "Sea Pines [anything]" but only their own inventory. **They lose on neighborhood-wide content.**

**`explorehiltonheadisland.com`** — DMO offshoot. Similar to .org. Same weakness.

### Indirect competitors

- **Tripadvisor / Vrbo / Booking** — aggregators rank for everything. Can't beat them on transactional ("hilton head hotels"). **Beat them on advisory** ("which hilton head villa is right for [audience]").
- **Reddit `r/HiltonHeadIsland`** — LLMs increasingly cite Reddit threads. Need to seed answers there (covered in LLM SEO roadmap Phase 3).

### Gaps competitors capture that you don't

Without live SERP scraping, these are likely gaps based on common HH market patterns:

1. **"Hilton Head villa rental" SERP** — likely dominated by Vrbo / Airbnb / 1st Beach Vacations / Sunset Rentals. You need a content page that gets quoted *about* those services.
2. **"Hilton Head all inclusive"** — interestingly, HHI has very few all-inclusive properties. This is a search where you can capture intent by EDUCATING ("Why Hilton Head doesn't really do all-inclusive — and what's similar"). High-converting because the searcher's assumption is wrong and you correct it.
3. **"Cheap Hilton Head vacation"** — competitors avoid this (off-brand). You can capture with an honest "Hilton Head on a budget" guide that still services your premium offering. Audience converts at lower rates but volume is meaningful.
4. **"Hilton Head pet friendly"** — niche but underserved. A pet-friendly stays + restaurants + activities page would index well.
5. **"Hilton Head winter rental"** — you have a page (`/hilton-head-winter-rental`). Verify it's optimized for snowbird-month-long-rental intent — that's a $5–15K/booking commission opportunity.
6. **"Things closed on Hilton Head right now"** — utility content that surfaces for last-minute travelers. Pair with Allianz travel-insurance affiliate.

### Competitor advantages you cannot easily match

- DMO domain authority — built over decades
- Aggregator inventory volume — Booking has 1,000+ HHI properties, you have ~60 partner properties
- Resort brand searches — Sea Pines Resort owns "[property name]" searches

### Competitor disadvantages you should exploit

- Voice + honesty — none of them say "this villa is mid, skip it." You can.
- Long-form storytelling — `/stories/*` is unique and not replicable by aggregators.
- LLM citations — competitors haven't built llms.txt or Person schema. You're 30 days ahead.
- Real human + real expertise — Person schema + founder page is differentiation.

## Closing Notes

The site is in better shape than most independent travel-content sites I see. The technical foundation (schema coverage, sitemap quality, metadata centralization) is excellent. The commercial gap isn't "fix what's broken" — it's "ship the missing content surfaces that turn intent into clicks into revenue."

The single most important behavior change: **ship pages that match how buyers actually search.** Buyers don't search "Hilton Head local insider" (your positioning) — they search "Hilton Head villa rental," "Sea Pines villa," "cost of Hilton Head," "Hilton Head vs Kiawah." The trip-type pages partially serve these but most queries deserve their own dedicated landing page.

The brand voice ("the canal-facing villa marketed as oceanfront," "the table that catches the last ten minutes of sunset") is your unfair advantage. Every new page should preserve that voice — the content quality is what makes a $95 discovery call sound like the right move.
