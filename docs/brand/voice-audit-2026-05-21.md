# Brand Voice Audit — Electrifying HiltonAhead.com

**Date:** 2026-05-21
**Goal:** Keep the editorial-magazine restraint that makes the brand credible. Dial up sensory atmosphere, time-of-day specificity, and body feeling. Make a reader physically picture themselves there inside three sentences.

---

## Verdict

**Strengths.** The current voice has real bones. The editorial cadence (`Dispatch № 12`), the italic display cuts, the dry insider asides ("the 7 p.m. table at Skull Creek the one that was somehow fully booked when you called"), the anti-marketing posture — all of that is gold. Do not flatten it into Buzzfeed enthusiasm.

**Weakness.** The copy is **visual + factual** when it should also be **sensory + emotional**. Right now most pages read like a well-written catalog. Catalogs inform. They don't make someone book.

**The fix in one sentence.** Add body-feeling — warmth, light, sound, salt, the exhale you take when you take the watch off — without sacrificing one ounce of editorial cool.

## The brand voice — codified

Use this on every revision. Pin to /docs/brand.

### Personality
A 47-year-old island local with a New Yorker subscription and salt in their hair. Hosts the table you want to be at. Doesn't try to sell you the island — knows the island already sold itself the second you saw it from the bridge.

### Voice attributes (on the spectrums from the framework)

| Attribute | Position | What this means |
|---|---|---|
| Formality | Editorial-casual | Long-form magazine, not blog post. Contractions yes, exclamation marks almost never. |
| Authority | Quiet expert | We've earned it; we don't announce it. |
| Emotion | Warm and *sensory* | This is the dial that needs turning up. |
| Complexity | Plain words, specific nouns | "Hard-packed sand," not "premier beachfront." |
| Energy | Slow-burn, not loud | Match the island. Unhurried wins. |
| Humor | Dry, occasional, never goofy | The aside, not the punchline. |

### The four rules of an electrified Hilton Ahead sentence

1. **Engage two senses, not just sight.** Sound, warmth, salt, smell of pluff mud at low tide.
2. **Anchor to a time of day.** "First light." "The hour the trolley stops running." "7:14 p.m., late June." Specificity is sexier than superlative.
3. **Put the reader's body in the scene.** Barefoot. The watch off. The salt drying on the back of your neck.
4. **Imply the story, don't narrate it.** The reader does half the work. That's what makes it stick.

### Words to use more

salt, slow, late, oak, light, marsh, first, last, almost, quiet, barefoot, pluff, glassy, hush, low-and-slow, golden, warm, dripping, sun-bleached, well-worn, sandy, hard-packed, fading, opening, closing, the one with…

### Words to retire (or use sparingly)

best, top, premier, ultimate, world-class, expert, curated (overused), exclusive, luxury, paradise, dream, unforgettable, amazing, stunning. *Especially "curated" — every Hilton Head site says it.*

## High-impact rewrites

### 1. Homepage hero `lede` — `data/hero.ts`

**Before**
> Villa picks. Tee times at Harbour Town. The 7 p.m. table at Skull Creek (the one that was somehow fully booked when you called). The short list of things worth doing and the longer list of things worth skipping. We plan Hilton Head the way we plan it for family, then hand it to you.

**Analysis.** This is the strongest single piece of copy on the site already. The asides work. It's a list of *deliverables*, though — not an *experience*. Add one breath of atmosphere before the deliverables and a closer that lands in the body.

**After**
> The island is twelve miles long and you have seven days. Somewhere in there is the villa with the porch facing the right way, the 7 p.m. table at Skull Creek (the one that was somehow fully booked when you called), and the tee time at Harbour Town that opens up two Tuesdays before your trip if you know to ask. We plan it the way we plan it for family — every reservation, every drive time, every quiet half-hour — and hand it to you. You arrive barefoot by Tuesday.

**Why it works.** Opens with a stake ("twelve miles, seven days, somewhere in there"). Keeps the original asides. Closes with a body-image (barefoot) on a specific day (Tuesday). The reader is already there.

### 2. Display headline — `hero.title`

**Before**
> The Hilton Head *you thought only* locals knew.

**Analysis.** Solid. The italic mid-line works. Mild "you'd never know" framing. Can be slightly more sensual.

**After (option A — keep current, very minor lift)**
> The Hilton Head *the locals* keep to themselves.

**After (option B — bolder)**
> The Hilton Head *that only opens* after the bridge.

The bridge image is iconic for HHI — the moment your phone signal drops, the live oaks close over the car, and a different rhythm starts. Use it.

### 3. `whyIsland.lede` — already strong, light polish

**Before**
> Hilton Head is the rare American resort island that kept its trees. No neon, no billboards. No high-rise crush either, because the building code never let one go up. Live oaks dripping Spanish moss, sixty-odd miles of bike path, and a coastline shaped by the Atlantic rather than by developers. The Lowcountry does unhurried better than anywhere else in the South.

**After**
> Hilton Head is the rare American resort island that kept its trees. No neon, no billboards, no high-rise crush — the building code never let one go up. Live oaks dripping Spanish moss, sixty-odd miles of bike path under that canopy, a coastline shaped by the Atlantic rather than by developers. By the second morning your shoulders drop two inches and you stop checking the time. The Lowcountry does unhurried better than anywhere else in the South.

**Why.** Inserts the body-line ("shoulders drop two inches… stop checking the time") that converts editorial observation into felt experience. Same length, twice the pull.

### 4. Pillar — "The beach, honestly"

**Before**
> Twelve miles of hard-packed sand you can bike on at low tide. No rocks, no undertow, no condo towers. The Atlantic here is forgiving. Shallow for fifty yards out, warm by mid-May, glassy most mornings.

**Analysis.** Good list. Add one moment.

**After**
> Twelve miles of hard-packed sand you can bike on at low tide — pedal a mile north of Coligny and you'll have it almost to yourself. No rocks, no undertow, no condo towers. The Atlantic here is forgiving: shallow for fifty yards out, warm by mid-May, glassy by 6:30 a.m. You'll keep going back at sunrise even on the days you swore you'd sleep in.

**Why.** Specific direction ("a mile north of Coligny"). Specific hour ("6:30 a.m.") instead of "most mornings." Behavior prediction at the end ("even on the days you swore you'd sleep in") — that's the line that makes someone laugh and forward the page.

### 5. Pillar — "The Lowcountry palette"

**Before**
> Moss, marsh, and magnolia. Shrimp off the boat at lunch, if you know which dock. Oysters at sunset. Bourbon on a porch after. There's a reason people who come once tend to come back.

**Analysis.** Almost perfect. One small turn lifts it.

**After**
> Moss, marsh, and magnolia. Shrimp off the boat at lunch — if you know which dock. Oysters at sunset, opened in front of you on a slab of pine. Bourbon on a porch after, while the cicadas start. There's a reason the people who come once start checking flights home before the bourbon's finished.

**Why.** Adds two sensory details (pine slab, cicadas) and replaces the polite closing line with a punchier behavior prediction ("checking flights home before the bourbon's finished"). Same restraint, sharper edge.

### 7. `brand.shortDescription` — `data/brand.ts`

**Before**
> A locally-run travel consulting service for Hilton Head. Villas, tee times, dinner reservations, and the 10 things only locals know about.

**After**
> A locally-run travel consultancy for Hilton Head Island. Villas chosen by porch and shade. Tee times sequenced by tide. Dinner reservations at the tables that catch the last ten minutes of light. The trip you'd plan if you'd lived here for thirty years.

**Why.** Each clause demonstrates *how* we work, not just what we do. "Chosen by porch and shade" tells you we drive past the villas before recommending them. "Sequenced by tide" tells you we know the courses well enough to play with weather. "Tables that catch the last ten minutes of light" puts a sunset in your head before you've read the next sentence.

### 9. FAQ #1 — homepage

**Before — Q:** Why hire a travel consultant for Hilton Head?
**Before — A:**
> Because the best of Hilton Head is never on the first page of Google. We know which oceanfront villa has the quiet pool (the one that fills up ten minutes after the cover comes off in March). We know which Skull Creek table gets the sunset view. And we know which Harbour Town tee time actually opens up two weeks out, even when the Resy page tells you otherwise. You get a trip without the research tax.

**Analysis.** Strong already. Tighten the open, sharpen the close, add one body image.

**After**
> Because the best of Hilton Head is never on the first page of Google — it's in the heads of the people who live here. We know which oceanfront villa has the pool that goes quiet by 4 p.m. (the one that fills up ten minutes after the cover comes off in March). We know which Skull Creek table catches the last ten minutes of sun in late July. And we know which Harbour Town tee time opens up two Tuesdays out, even when the booking page tells you otherwise. The result: a trip your spouse thinks took a weekend to plan, that actually took thirty years.

**Why.** New hook line ("in the heads of the people who live here") states the value prop in one image. "Goes quiet by 4 p.m." is specific. The closing line is the soundbite — it's the sentence we want quoted back to us in a testimonial.

### 10. FAQ #3 — pricing answer (anti-marketing strength)

**Before**
> Four tiers: a $95 discovery session (credited back if you book), $450 flat for a custom itinerary, 8% of trip total (minimum $800) for full signature service with on-island concierge, and 12% (minimum $2,500) for groups and weddings. Every engagement is quoted up front. No surprise fees, no kickbacks baked into your rates.

**Analysis.** This is *good*. Pricing answers earn the trust. Don't electrify it — that would ruin it. Make one small structural improvement: open with the principle, then the tiers.

**After**
> We're paid by you, not by the properties — that's the whole reason we can recommend the right villa instead of the most-commissioned one. Four tiers: $95 discovery session (credited back if you book), $450 flat for a custom itinerary, 8% of trip total (min $800) for signature service with on-island concierge, and 12% (min $2,500) for groups and weddings. Every engagement is quoted up front. No surprise fees, no kickbacks.

**Why.** Lead with the principle, not the table. Buyers in this segment read the *first sentence* of a pricing answer — make it the one that earns trust, not the one that lists numbers.

### 11. Founder bio — `data/founder.ts`

**Before**
> William Griffith is the founder of Hilton Ahead Travel Co., a Hilton Head Island travel consultancy. A full-time island resident, William plans every trip himself — drives past the villas before recommending them, knows which Sea Pines bike path floods after an August storm, and keeps a working list of which Skull Creek tables catch the last ten minutes of sunset in late July. The practice is intentionally small: one trip at a time, by hand, no franchise, no call center, no scripts.

**Analysis.** Already strong. One small lift in the open turns "competent founder bio" into "founder bio someone screenshots."

**After**
> William Griffith plans your Hilton Head trip from a porch on Hilton Head. A thirty-year island resident, he drives past the villas before recommending them, knows which Sea Pines bike path floods after an August storm, and keeps a list — yes, an actual list — of which Skull Creek tables catch the last ten minutes of sunset in late July. The practice is intentionally small: one trip at a time, by hand. No franchise. No call center. No scripts. The work is the work.

**Why.** Opens with the image ("from a porch on Hilton Head") instead of a corporate-title cadence. "Yes, an actual list" lands the dry wit. "The work is the work" closes with a line that sticks — and earns the founder credibility for the rest of the page.

### 13. Golf packages hook — `data/tripTypes.ts`

**Before**
> Eight golf courses inside fifteen minutes, three of them national Top-100. We sequence the Harbour Town tee times, match the group to the right villa, and land the whole party at first tee with time to stretch.

**Analysis.** Pure feature list. For a golf trip — which is high-AOV and emotional — this should *put the reader on the first tee.*

**After**
> Eight golf courses inside fifteen minutes — three of them national Top-100 — and the group's biggest decision becomes which one to play Wednesday. We sequence the Harbour Town tee times by tide and crowd, match the foursome to a villa inside five minutes of the first tee, and have your bags in the cart by 7:42 a.m. for an 8:00 push. You're stretching at the range before the first group on Course 18 has reached the par-3.

**Why.** Same facts, but now the reader is *on the first tee at 7:42 a.m.* with their bag already loaded, not reading a marketing bullet. Golf trips are sold by visualization. Visualize.

### 16. Blog post intros — universal pattern

Most blog posts open with a category statement ("Hilton Head has eight courses…"). Every post should instead open with a **moment**.

**Pattern: First three lines of every blog post**

1. **One sentence putting the reader in a moment.** Time, place, body.
2. **One sentence stating the problem the post solves.**
3. **One sentence promising the payoff in concrete terms.**

**Example — for the hypothetical post "Best month to visit Hilton Head"**

> It's 71° on Hilton Head right now and there's no one on the beach. It's also a Wednesday in October, which is the answer most people don't think to ask. Here's the month-by-month read on weather, crowds, and rates — written by someone who's lived through thirty Octobers here.

## Legal/compliance flags

- The line *"We don't take kickbacks from villas, resorts, or restaurants"* in FAQ #pricing is a strong differentiator and reads as compliant — keep it.
- Founder bio claim "thirty-year island resident" — confirm the actual number; `data/founder.ts` currently has `yearsOnIsland: 30`. If accurate, use the number everywhere. If approximate, use "nearly thirty years."
- The "**8–12% below public listings**" claim in FAQ #pricing should have a small footnote linking to a representative example, or be softened to "often below" to avoid an unsubstantiated-savings claim under FTC endorsement rules.

---

## What NOT to change

- The editorial scaffolding (`Dispatch №`, `Spring 2026`, `№ 01` section numbers, italic mid-headline cuts). This is the design system's biggest brand asset. Touch nothing.
- The anti-marketing voice in the FAQs. The pricing answer is your most-converting page in the funnel. Do not dress it up.
- The proof line ("255+ trips advised, Locally based, Hilton Head Island"). Quantified, modest, true.
- Brand color, fonts, photography crop standards. Visual identity is doing its job — verbal identity is the lift.
