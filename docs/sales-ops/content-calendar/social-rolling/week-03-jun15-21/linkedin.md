### #2 — Thursday Jun 18 — Operator note (walk-to-beach)
- **Hook:** I walked 12 Hilton Head villa listings last month with my phone in my pocket. Apple Health says the listings lied 9 of 12 times.
- **Body:**
  ```
  Walk-to-beach claims on Hilton Head villa rentals are a category-wide credibility problem. Here are the actual numbers from my walks:

  Sea Pines South Beach Pavilion villas — listings say "3 minutes to beach." Real walks: 7-14 minutes depending on building. The only honest category is the South Beach Marina condos.

  Palmetto Dunes oceanfront row — listings say "0 minutes." Real walks: 1-3 minutes door to sand. Honest.

  Palmetto Dunes lagoon-front — listings say "5 minutes by cart." Real walks: 12-18 minutes on foot, 4-6 by cart. The cart is required in July or the walk in the heat is punishment.

  Forest Beach — wildly inconsistent. Some are honestly 3-4 minutes. Some claim "walk to beach" and are 12 minutes through Coligny Plaza traffic.

  Shelter Cove — listings claiming "walk to beach" are flat wrong. There is no walk to beach. Everyone drives or shuttles.

  Three implications for travel planners and trip-research-savvy clients:

  1. Distance claims without "by foot" or "by cart" specification are marketing copy, not measurement. Treat accordingly.

  2. The honest category is "by cart, X minutes" or "by foot, X minutes." Anything else, walk it yourself or ask someone who has.

  3. We log actual walk times for every partner villa in our portfolio. It's a small operational cost. It's the single most repeated piece of client feedback we get: "the walk was actually accurate." That's a low bar that the industry doesn't clear.

  The industry-level fix is simple. The market hasn't enforced it. Until it does, the operational discipline pays.
  ```

### #3 — Sunday Jun 21 — Industry observation (the Shelter Cove case)
- **Hook:** The most underrated neighborhood in our portfolio is also the cheapest one. Worth thinking about why.
- **Body:**
  ```
  Shelter Cove Marina on Hilton Head is the neighborhood the brochure photography doesn't capture and the algorithm doesn't surface. It is also one of two neighborhoods where we have the highest rebook rate among new clients (the other is Forest Beach).

  Peak July 2BR nightly: $300-600. Marina-side. Walkable to Hudson's, Charbar, Black Marlin Bayside Grill, San Miguel's, a bookstore, ice cream. No private beach (which is the framing problem — clients assume they need beach-from-the-balcony when they actually need a beach 4 minutes away and a walkable dinner scene).

  The pattern in our data: clients who book Shelter Cove for the first time say "we'll try it for a year then move to Sea Pines next year." Then they rebook Shelter Cove. The retention beats Sea Pines retention by a meaningful margin in the couples / shoulder-season-snowbird segment.

  Why this matters beyond Shelter Cove:

  1. The brand-name premium is highest in the planning phase and lowest in the post-trip-recall phase. People stop caring about the gated entrance by Day 3.

  2. Walkable dining matters more than brochure photography for repeat bookings. A Sea Pines villa with a 12-minute drive to dinner loses to a Shelter Cove condo with a 4-minute walk to Hudson's, every single time, in the post-trip survey.

  3. The market is undersupplying walkable urbanism in resort travel because the photography doesn't sell as well as the gated/manicured photography. There's an arbitrage here for operators who can build the right narrative.

  The Shelter Cove segment is small. The lesson generalizes. The cheapest option in your portfolio is sometimes the one that retains the best.
  ```
