## Why this platform fits (or doesn't)
LinkedIn is a sleeper-fit channel that almost no Hilton Head travel brand uses. Our $200K+ HHI buyer is the LinkedIn buyer — exec recruiters, finance, law, consulting, biotech, and professional services. The audience that books Palmetto Bluff weddings, plans corporate retreats at Sea Pines, and snowbirds in Florida is on LinkedIn daily and never sees travel content there. We can dominate the niche by simply showing up with locally-knowledgeable, first-person observations. Personal profile leads, company page supports.

## Platform-specific gotchas
- Don't post obvious sales content from the personal profile — LinkedIn punishes it. "Plan a trip" CTAs are fine; "Click here to book now" CTAs aren't.
- Don't @-tag people who don't engage with your content. LinkedIn's algorithm watches for @-spam and downranks repeat offenders.
- Don't share content from the company page first — share from the personal profile, then reshare from the company page. LinkedIn weights personal-profile reach 3-4x higher.
- Don't use third-person language ("Hilton Ahead Travel Co provides..."). Use first-person. The platform is built for human voices.
- Don't post on Friday afternoons or weekends except Sunday evening. Reach drops 60%+.
- Don't put external URLs in the post body — LinkedIn suppresses link-in-post reach. Put the URL in the first comment or in the post's image as text.

## Compliance / FTC
- Posts featuring affiliate links (Booking.com, Vrbo, Amazon) require "#ad" or "Some links may be affiliate — we may earn a small commission at no cost to you" in the post body.
- Sponsored partnership posts must use LinkedIn's "Paid Partnership" disclosure (settings → professional → branded content).
- Corporate retreat or wedding-planning case studies that name specific clients require written client permission — anonymize otherwise.

## Tone rules
- Never use exclamation marks except for emphasis on a rare specific point.
- Never start a post with "Looking for..." or "Have you ever..."

- LinkedIn rewards thoughtful, declarative, numbers-supported writing. Lead with the operating-business reality, not the marketing veneer.
- Posts under 1,300 characters perform best for engagement. Articles over 1,500 words perform best for impressions over time.
