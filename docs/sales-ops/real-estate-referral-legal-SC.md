# Real Estate Referral Fees in South Carolina — Legal Feasibility

**Date:** 2026-06-02
**Question:** Can Hilton Ahead (William, **not** a licensed real estate agent) collect a per-closing real estate referral fee in SC?
**Short answer:** **No — not while unlicensed.** But there are two legal paths to the same money.

> ⚠️ **Not legal advice.** This is web-sourced research to inform your brokerage calls and an attorney check. Confirm with a SC real estate attorney and the SC LLR Real Estate Commission (803-896-4400) before signing anything.

---

## The verdict: unlicensed per-closing referral fee = ILLEGAL in SC

Two layers of law both block it:

### 1. SC state law (Title 40, Ch. 57)
- **§40-57-710(A)(12):** the Real Estate Commission may discipline/revoke a licensee who *"pays a commission or compensation to an unlicensed individual for activities requiring a license."*
- A licensee **may not pay a referral fee or finder's fee to an unlicensed individual** who isn't a party to the transaction.
- **Soliciting a referral fee itself requires a license.**
- SC's referral-fee exception is **licensed-party-to-licensed-party only** (e.g., a resident licensee paying an out-of-state brokerage). Unlicensed persons are excluded.

→ A brokerage that paid you a per-closing referral fee would be **risking its own license.** Most won't, and the ones that would are a red flag.

### 2. Federal RESPA (overrides state law)
- Almost all home purchases involve a **federally-related mortgage** → RESPA applies.
- RESPA §8 bars any *"fee, kickback, or thing of value"* for referring **settlement-service** business to anyone not licensed/performing the service.
- **RESPA preempts** even state finder's-fee exceptions. A couple of states (CA, KS) allow a narrow "raw referral" fee *only* if it's **not contingent on closing** and the person doesn't solicit — **SC has no such carve-out.**

**Bottom line:** the clean $9k-per-closing version of the whale is **not legal as an unlicensed referrer.**

## Two legal paths to the same money

### PATH A — Get a SC referral license (unlocks the real $9k/closing) ✅ recommended
The cheap, standard fix. Hold a SC real estate license at a **referral-only brokerage** → broker-to-broker referral fees become **fully legal.**

- **What it takes:** SC real estate (Associate) license — **~90 hrs pre-licensing education + state exam + background check.** *(verify current hours/fees with SC LLR.)*
- **Then:** park the license at a referral brokerage (e.g., **ReferSC.com ~$45/yr**, Pinnacle). As a referral agent you **can't do agency work** (no representing clients) — which is exactly what you want; you only refer.
- **Cost:** ~**$500–$800** all-in (course + exam + license + background) + ~$45–100/yr brokerage. **One $1.5M closing (~$9k) pays for it ~12×.**
- **Timeline:** realistically **4–10 weeks** to licensed (online self-paced course + exam scheduling), then refer, then a 30–90 day closing.
- **Implication:** this is a **next-quarter cash** play, not this-quarter. **Start the course now** so closings land in Q3.

### PATH B — No license: sell advertising / leads, NOT referrals (legal now, weaker)
You **can** legally take money from agents/brokerages if it's **advertising or lead-gen that is NOT contingent on a closing** (this is how Zillow/realtor.com charge agents):
- A **flat monthly sponsorship** to be the "recommended agent" on `/move-to-hilton-head` + `/sell-or-rent-your-villa`, or
- A **flat per-lead fee** (pay-per-introduction), **never per-closing.**
- ⚠️ Must be genuinely **non-contingent + disclosed**; paper it with an attorney so it can't be recharacterized as a disguised referral fee.
- **Reality check:** at **~100 organic clicks/month**, a brokerage won't pay much for featured placement on a low-traffic page. So Path B is **small until traffic grows** — more of a later/recurring play than a this-quarter mover.

## What this changes in the 90-day plan

1. **The real estate whale is real but gated behind a license** → it's a **Q3 cash play, not this quarter.** Action this quarter: **enroll in the SC pre-licensing course now** (highest-ROI setup move; pays for itself on the first closing).
2. **Do NOT** accept an unlicensed per-closing referral fee, even if a brokerage offers — it's their license on the line and your liability.
3. **Reweight this-quarter cash** to the **license-free, traffic-independent** plays: **concierge planning fees + destination weddings + corporate/golf-group retreats** (sold via network/outbound). Those carry Q2.
4. Revised brokerage call: ask *"I'm getting my SC referral license — will you take referrals broker-to-broker once I'm parked at a referral brokerage, at 25%?"* (legal framing) instead of asking them to pay an unlicensed person.
